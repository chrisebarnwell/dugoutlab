Help your kid love the game.

Backyard games that take ten minutes, the words that help after a tough game, and how to support a young player without adding pressure.

Play together this week

Ten minutes in the backyard

Pick one. Keep it short, keep score if they like scores, and stop while they still want more.

Try this with your kid

Hoop points

Hang a hula hoop on a net or fence. Ten swings off a tee or soft toss. Ball through the hoop is a point. Write the score down and try to beat it next time.

10 min · a hoop, a tee, soft balls
Why a target helps
Try this with your kid

Call your shot

Before each swing, your kid points: left, middle, or right. Ten balls. A ball that goes where they called counts. The point is the plan, not the power.

10 min · any ball, any bat
Play it with scoring on a phone
Try this with your kid

The nine-spot tee

Set the tee low and away, then work across the plate: outside, middle, inside, each one out front, even, and deep. One swing at each spot. Wiffle balls tell the truth about contact.

10 min · tee, Wiffle balls · ages 9+
Where this routine comes from
Try this with your kid

Where are you going to hit it?

The night before a game, your kid draws a field and circles where they want to hit the ball. They say it out loud once, then the card goes in their back pocket.

5 min · paper, a pencil

In the stands

Six words that help more than any tip

"I love to watch you play."

Say it first after the game, win or lose. During the game, cheer effort and cheer other kids by name. Let the coach be the one voice on the field.

Read The Sideline That Helps
Try this at the next game
  1. Pick your six words ahead of time and say them first.
  2. Cheer three kids who are not yours, by name.
  3. Swap every instruction for a cheer.
  4. On the ride home, ask one question and stop: "What was the most fun part today?"
